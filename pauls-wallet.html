The story so far (as told by the people he keeps beating)

Dan Ward grew up in a time and place where vulnerability was a dirty word, with too many syllables to be one of the cool ones. Thankfully, so was "quizzing", so he kept that quiet as well.

Every great comedian needs a hero. Dan's is Paul Sinha: The Sinnerman, Chaser on The Chase, stand-up comedian, and proof that knowing an absurd amount of facts and making a room laugh can be done by the same person. Paul Sinha got there through years on the circuit and a seat on the Chasers' bench.

Dan took a different route: Paul's Lockdown Quiz, the world-renowned online quiz, beamed live to a global audience of almost a dozen households. He won it. Then he won it again. Then he kept on winning it, week after week, and not always cheating!

When serious illness left Dan "fighting somewhat feebly for his life" (Steph Anderson, April 2022), he was fighting anything but feebly for top spot on the leaderboard. Hospital bed, wires everywhere, a heart transplant on the way, and his answers were still in before the clock hit ten. The nurses thought the beeping was his monitors. It was the buzzer.

He got a brand new heart. Everyone else lost heart when they couldn't even get a week to win the quizzes without him dialling in from his bed. Selfish! (Joe Wotherspoon, Nov 2022)

Now Dan has written a show about manhood, mortality and rebirth. "Man Down", says the official blurb, is "basically Rudyard Kipling's poem 'If', if it was an hour long, more working class & arguably better." We've heard the running order. There's a lot about strength. There's a lot about masculinity. There is not one single mention of the life-saving power of a Friday night quiz. Kipling would have put it in. If you can keep your head when all about you are losing theirs, and still know the capital of Burkina Faso…

His real disability

Some comedians overcome the odds. Dan supports Sunderland and is trying to make it on the Newcastle comedy scene.

The Stand is on High Bridge, about a ten-minute walk from St James' Park. He's chosen to do his debut solo show in the heart of enemy territory, in front of a Toon crowd, on purpose. Medically, he's been through worse. Emotionally, this might be the bravest thing he's ever done.

Reasonable adjustments have been made. Nobody will mention their time in League One. Probably.

The serious bit

Man Down is Dan's debut solo show: a coming-of-middle-age story about manhood, mortality and rebirth. It's directed by stand-up and comedy director Lee Kyle as part of The Stand's New Directions, which backs promising comedy talent in the North East.

He's been to the edge and come back with an hour of stand-up. Go and give him the biggest laugh of his life. He's earned it. And if he brings up his quiz record in the bar afterwards, you're allowed to leave.
